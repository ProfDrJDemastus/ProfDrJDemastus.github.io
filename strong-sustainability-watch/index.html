How this works

Every Monday a script on GitHub queries OpenAlex for articles and reviews published in the journals on the allow-list, querying each journal ISSN separately and looking back 45 days so late-indexed articles are caught. The list goes back to articles published from 1 January 2020. Each article gets a relevance score from 0 to 10, where 10 is most relevant. Core concepts in the title count most and core concepts in the abstract or keywords count next. Breadth across topic categories adds a little, purely weak-sustainability or ESG framing is penalized, and each matched matrix theory adds 0.75, up to 1.5. Articles scoring 3 or more join the list.

An article is linked to a theory when its title, abstract or keywords use that theory's distinctive language, or when its reference list cites one of the theory's anchor works from the sustainability matrix article. The reason is shown under each article. Matching is by keywords and citations, not by reading, so treat the links as leads to check.

The settings live on GitHub in three files, the journal list, the theories and anchor works, and the scoring dictionary. The full data is in articles.json.
